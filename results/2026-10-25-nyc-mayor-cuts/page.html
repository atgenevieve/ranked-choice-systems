NYC mayoral primaries

How many ranks matter in a race shaped like New York City's Democratic primary for mayor: 1,000 simulated races, next to what the real 2025 and 2021 ballots show when they're cut short.

How many ranks matter in a mayoral primary

Simulated results are based on 1,000 simulations of each race, with the real race's candidates and turnout, one seat, and a ballot that allows up to 5 ranks, counted by moving each vote down to the voter's next choice until one candidate has a majority. Voters follow the project's public-voter model, fitted to 1,100 Scottish council elections. Real results come from recounting every ballot in the NYC Board of Elections' cast vote records with each ballot cut after rank 1 to 5.

One caution: simulated voters run out of choices more often than real ones. By the final round, about 25% of simulated ballots have no candidate left, against 5% in the real 2025 race and 15% in 2021. The model's public voters were fitted to Scottish ballots, and NYC mayoral voters rank more of the candidates, so treat the simulated curves as a rough guide for NYC. The real races and all 126 real NYC primaries are the firmer evidence.
